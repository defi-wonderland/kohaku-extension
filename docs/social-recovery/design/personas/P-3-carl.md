# Carl, a passkey and one friend

Carl runs a small business. His friend Sara got him into crypto last year, and for anything technical she is who he calls. He has an iPhone and a MacBook, both on iCloud, no hardware wallet and no other crypto contact. He is not technical and does not want to become so. He buys ether through an exchange app Sara set up for him, and he has sent transactions from his account before.

He holds a synced passkey and one relationship the design touches: Sara's wallet, an ordinary key she controls. Sara is the guardian fixture `P-6-sara.md`. He stands to lose the account if his laptop dies while Sara is unreachable, and the setup copy tells him both must answer and losing either locks him out.

## Journey, setup

Carl picks the preset your device and your guardians on the presets screen (C-01), reads there that a first-release recovery is paid by his own key and that saving is one transaction his account's key pays, then edits the preset down to his real inventory: his passkey and Sara, both required, and the editor shows the line that both must answer, losing either locks him out, beside the sizing rule's own line that a group of any one of two is the shape it prefers at two items, which he reads and keeps his two rows anyway.

He enrolls the passkey on his Mac and runs its test, the tested state drawn on C-05 as a phone passkey while his own row reads MacBook passkey, synced as its backup flags report (C-05a, C-05), enters Sara's address after asking her and reads that if her address is a smart account whoever controls it can approve for it, and that a recovery which uses her puts her address on chain in the clear, that even unused anyone who can guess it can find it, and to ask her first (C-05c). Her row reads not tested.

He keeps the 48 hour waiting period (C-06), keeps the default privacy level and sets the recovery password (C-06e). The review leads with two required rows, the line that both must answer, the waiting period and the cost line, and under verify the details the key method's row, audited with no outside party, and the word that nobody can stop either of his methods, since neither the passkey method nor the key method carries a pause (C-07f). He saves (C-07e) and prints the card, keeping it at his shop (C-08).

## Journey, recovery

Carl's laptop is lost. His phone still holds the synced passkey. He installs the extension on a new laptop, takes the fast track (A-00 to A1-03), pastes the address from his card (D-02b), confirms the account (D-04d), enters the recovery password, and sees two rows (D-06f, D-06). The gas step asks him to fund his new key, and he sends it ether (A1-04). He completes the passkey row from his phone (D-07b, D-07).

His row tells him to copy the approval page's link with its message and send it to her over the channel they already use, and the message asks her to call him back on a number she already has. When she calls he reads her the account and the new key.

She opens it in a browser with Kohaku, reads the account, the new key, the key being removed, the words no payment, the deadline and the setup number, compares the account and the new key with what he read to her, signs on the page with the wallet installed in that browser, the route D-314 tests on the first day, and sends the one-line approval back as the page tells her (E1-01, E1-04, E1-05).

Carl pastes her approval and the checklist counts it on paste and checks it the way the chain will (D-08), showing the request's remaining validity, the wallet's own default of 24 hours from its creation.

He meets no gas step before the submission, since the key he funded at A1-04 holds enough, reads the confirmation with the new key and the key being removed (D-11), submits from his new key (D-11b), waits 48 hours (D-13), executes from the same key when the countdown reads execution due, and finishes on the done screen, which says his synced passkey may still follow the account that syncs it, for him his Apple account, states the repair order, secure that account first, add a fresh credential that does not sync to it, then remove the old row, and says that removing one of his two rows would leave the other as the whole rule (D-15d).

Carl's lost laptop still holds the account's key and his synced passkey, so whoever finds it can act while his own waiting period runs. That holder could use the account's key to cancel Carl's attempt, and could not complete a competing recovery, since Carl's path needs Sara's approval too. The confirmation and the countdown Carl reads say that only this account's own key can cancel this recovery before it finishes, the key his lost laptop still holds (D-11, D-13). If his attempt is cancelled he reads the cancelled terminal (D-14). It says the account's own key cancelled it, which may be the lost device, and he gathers the two approvals again.
