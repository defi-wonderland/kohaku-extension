Part B runs the Aadhaar method for real: the Anon Aadhaar prover in the extension page (the artifacts fetched from the project's server, 296 MB in gzip chunks on the first run of a profile, then cached; a proof takes about a minute), the SDK's own checks (a QR older than a day is refused after proving; the config carries a 24-hour freshness window; zero disclosures), and the chain: the access test at enrollment is judged by the deployed test-only Aadhaar method's verify view (a read), the setup is saved on Sepolia (a transaction the owner signs), the claim at recovery is proven in the page and submitted on Sepolia (a transaction the owner signs). The test-only method trusts Anon Aadhaar's public TEST key, so a test QR signed by that key verifies; a real Aadhaar QR is out of scope here.

## The hand-back protocol

You hand back (your final message) at four points and the coordinator resumes you with a message. Each hand-back names the point, the state of each daemon (pid, port, the page's route), where you are in the steps, and what the coordinator must do. Never wait on your own for the owner; never press Sign; never click anything in the wallet's sign window.

- HB-1 "funding": after both wallets exist (steps 1 and 2): the two basic addresses are in `walk/b/wallet-1.env` and `walk/b/wallet-2.env` (never in the message). The coordinator funds both keys and resumes you.
- HB-2 "sign the setup save": at the setup's sign step with the sign window open (step 5). The owner presses Sign. The coordinator resumes you; you then watch the write land and continue.
- HB-3 "sign the submission": at the recovery submission's sign step (step 8). As HB-2.
- HB-4 "sign the execution": after the wait period, at the execution's sign step (step 9). As HB-2. Then you finish and hand back the report.

If a step cannot be reached (a build failure, a prover that never finishes, a chain read that fails), hand back at once with the evidence instead of working around it; a workaround that changes the code is never yours.
